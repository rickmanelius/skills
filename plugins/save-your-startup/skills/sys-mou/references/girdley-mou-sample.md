## Page 1

**Memorandum of Understanding**

**Vision**
Build a big chain of neighborhood bar & grill restaurants in Texas and beyond. (Grilldleys.com)

**Organizational Design Principles**
- Should be able for ownership to supervise passively.
- Set up to “compound” where FCF is reinvested in the business
- Set up to run one experiment at a time to prove model with a minimum absolute risk

**Contributions/Responsibilities**

*Partner #1*
- Leverage existing land ownership
- One or more trial locations if we want them. Partner to negotiate 1 year free rent; then 9 YEAR options for us to continue there at reasonable rate after that (8% of sales?) [TALK ABOUT FAIR DEAL ON THIS IN ISSUE]
- Act as first line of defense if we must “get our hands dirty”
- Board Chair / attend board meetings
- $100k (50k in Tranche 1, 50k in Tranche 2). expected 60/40 split in $
- Hire our first GM and asst GM. (using a variation of my hiring process)
- It’s expected 20 hours a month will be required to get this started

*Partner #2*
- Construction expertise. It is expected to be a situation where we’ll order from one of their facilities. They should lead the process of choosing a GC for the first setup. Partner #1 and GM will help. After that, GM or CEO will own new site development with them acting in a consultative position only.
- Put in 2/3rds the time initially that Partner #1 does. Over time, we both will go totally passive except for Board roles.
- Naming / Branding design
- Board member / attend board meetings
- $60k (30k in Tranche 1, 30k in Tranche 2)
- It’s expected 12 hours a month will be required to get this started

**Company Structure**
- A federal C-corp (simpler to reinvest profits)
- 60% Partner #1/40% Partner #2 (in line with $ contributed and above structure)
- Board of both partners to supervise the management.
- Push-pull buy-sell agreement (either owner goes to his partner and offers to buy them out. Partner can either accept that offer or buy the first owner out under the same terms. 30 days to respond. 90 days to close.)

## Reading notes (ours, not Mike's)

**The structure.** Page 1 is the deal, in four blocks: Vision (one sentence), Organizational Design Principles (three bullets on how the company should run), Contributions/Responsibilities (one list per partner, mixing money, time, assets and jobs), and Company Structure (entity, split, governance, exit). Page 2 is the plan: each person's first big priorities. "Rocks" and "Accountability chart" are terms from EOS (the Entrepreneurial Operating System); a rock is one of the few things a person commits to finish in roughly the next quarter.

**What makes it work.**
- It is short. Page 1 is 365 words and fills the page; page 2 is a 54-word table.
- Money and time come as numbers: $100k and $60k, 20 and 12 hours a month, two tranches each.
- The exit is defined before anyone needs it, with a clock on it (30 days to respond, 90 to close).
- It names who leads what, and when a partner's role shrinks ("consultative position only").
- It leaves an open issue visible instead of pretending it is settled ("[TALK ABOUT FAIR DEAL ON THIS IN ISSUE]").

**Where `sys:mou` pushes further on clarity.** None of these are criticisms of Mike's process. The sample was a quick illustration, and they are exactly the kinds of slips a clarity pass exists to catch.
- **One number, stated once.** The split is "60/40 ... in line with $ contributed", but $100k and $60k is 62.5/37.5. "2/3rds the time" sits beside 20 and 12 hours a month, which is 60%. When one fact appears in two forms, the forms drift apart, and later each partner quotes the form they prefer.
- **Vague words get a number or an owner.** "Reasonable rate", "help", "first line of defense", "get our hands dirty" and "it's expected" read fine on the day and differently a year later.
- **Open issues live in one list**, each with an owner and a date, so none stays buried mid-bullet.
- **Contributions and responsibilities split apart.** What each person puts in (money, hours, assets) is a different question from what each person owns (decisions, outcomes). Mixing them in one list hides gaps.
- **Names, not "Partner #1".** In a real MOU, use people's names.
- **Each person's "why".** A reader asked under the post, "What do you expect to get out of it?" A partner who wants cash flow and one who wants a sale in five years can agree on every bullet above and still collide.
