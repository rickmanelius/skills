## What to check

1. **Disclosures of others' confidential information.** Did the user share a client's, employer's or partner's
   material with the counterparty, or the counterparty's material with anyone else? Search the session prompts,
   notes and shared folders for the names on the user's denylist.
2. **Client material in this work, and the reverse.** Code, data, designs or wording from a client engagement
   that appears in work for the counterparty, or counterparty material in client work. Check repos for shared
   files, copied functions and data files.
3. **Recordings.** Any meeting recording or transcript made without every party's consent where the law requires
   it. Note where each participant was.
4. **Tools holding the counterparty's material.** Personal cloud drives, AI services the material was pasted into,
   personal repos, synced notes. What would return-or-destroy require?
5. **Statements on the record.** Messages or posts that cut against the user's claims ("this is all your idea",
   "happy to give you this"). Better to know now.
6. **Signatures.** Anything the user signed without reading, or an agreement still not countersigned.
7. **Overlapping obligations.** A current employer's or client's agreement that may claim the same work.

## Severity

- **High:** could void a claim, breach another agreement, or needs a lawyer before signing.
- **Medium:** should be fixed or disclosed before signing.
- **Low:** worth tidying up; no action needed to sign.
